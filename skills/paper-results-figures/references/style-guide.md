# Shared Figure Style

These are the current defaults for manuscript result figures. Explicit user choices override them. New categories should reuse the applicable rules rather than introducing inconsistent formatting.

Apply these conventions in the reusable renderers, including layout-specific default resolution, rather than through cosmetic gallery overrides or a parent agent's repeated instructions. Omitted fields produce the documented baseline for the chosen category/layout and palette. Requested overlays, meaningful color/size encodings, hypotheses and scientific bounds remain explicit choices; their synthetic example values are not defaults for unrelated inputs. Adapt measured geometry after rendering when data content requires it, while preserving the selected scientific comparison and palette contract.

## Numbers and inference

- Display measured values, measured-quantity size-legend ticks, means, and test statistics with **three decimal places**, including trailing zeros. A continuous color axis follows the compact axis-tick rule below.
- Axis ticks use **compact adaptive decimals**: `0.5`, `1.0`, or `0.25` as appropriate, removing unnecessary trailing zeros while retaining at least one decimal place. Use `scripts/formatting.R` for consistent axis formatting across categories.
- The p-value significance threshold is the formatting exception: write `0.05`, not `0.050`. Nonsignificant numeric p-values retain three decimal places.
- Counts such as sample size, sample count or nucleotide count remain integers, including size-guide labels. Category names and identifiers are preserved verbatim.
- Make comparisons and significance decisions using unrounded values. Display `p-value < 0.05` for p < 0.05; otherwise display `p-value = 0.xxx`. Keep raw p-values and all calculations at full precision in machine-readable output.

## Physical size and typography

Design at final print size for a 180 mm manuscript row. Use `layout_columns: 3` for a default 57 mm panel (55–58 mm range), or `layout_columns: 4` for 42 mm (41–43 mm range). These leave 9 mm or 12 mm total row space for gutters. `width_mm` and `height_mm` explicitly set export dimensions and override legacy inch-valued `width` and `height`. On a resolved configuration, change `width_mm` directly when switching widths. A larger compound figure can occupy multiple panel slots when that allocation is explicitly established; record its complete width. Adjacent model/metric trend profiles otherwise share the single-figure canvas budget, rather than multiplying the width by the facet count.

Prefer width greater than height. Start near 57 × 46 mm for categorical panels and 57 × 54 mm for paired scatter or many horizontal categories. Width and height refer to the complete exported canvas, including titles, tags, ranking/significance annotations and axis text. Contact-sheet style labels such as “Violin + points + SD” are separate overview annotations; they are excluded from individual figure dimensions and individual exports. Fit compound content into the requested complete canvas first: share outer axis names, compress inter-panel gaps and guides, and select numeric ticks from actual panel dimensions. Do not enlarge the canvas merely because more models or metrics are present. Propose and record a larger allocation only when readable scientific content cannot fit. Height can grow when scientifically necessary and authorized; preserve the square data panel in paired scatter. Typography is specified at final size, not on an oversized canvas that will later be shrunk. Start with 7–8 pt titles/axis titles, 6–6.5 pt ticks/category names, and comparable numeric annotations. Long category lists may need about 5.6 pt labels, line wrapping, or a taller panel; inspect readability before delivery. Keep ticks about one point smaller than axis titles.

The multiple-model four-column profile starts at 42 × 38 mm, with method labels 5.7 pt, numeric values 1.9 mm, ranking text 1.8 mm, titles 7 pt, axis titles 6.5 pt and ticks 5.5 pt. Single-metric figures requesting significance start at 57 × 54 mm in the three-column profile. Compound layouts apply their own complete canvas sizes from [the multiple-metric specification](multi-metric-comparison.md#choose-the-layout), rather than scaling a single-panel screenshot. Explicit dimensions, font sizes and saved figure settings take precedence; the multiple-model statistics record retains the resolved `visual_profile`.

Use **actual Arial for every text element**. Resolve fonts, embed and verify PDF fonts, and embed Arial in editable SVG using `scripts/font_export.R`. PNG uses ragg. Do not substitute a generic sans serif.

For categorical method/value labels, default `label_angle`, `value_angle` and `label_wrap` to `auto`. Measure actual Arial text widths on the final-size device before choosing 0°, 45° or 90° rotation or method-name wrapping. `text_fit_gap_mm` starts at 0.7 mm and `text_min_panel_mm` at 12 mm. Horizontal method labels may wrap to retain useful panel width. Shared-row values remain outside by default; an explicit internal layout uses larger measured text and adjusts automatic baseline padding/view with readable rotation until every value fits. An impossible fixed geometry needs canvas refinement, not relocation of requested internal values outside. Explicit angle/wrapping choices take precedence. If text still cannot fit, report the required wider canvas or shorter display label rather than shrinking fonts or clipping. Keep the measured decision in the exported configuration and `statistics.text_fitting`.

Place an optional panel letter at the complete figure's left edge (including labels), at most slightly beyond it, and slightly above the title. Use bold Arial. Do not align it only to the inner data panel or leave a large detached tag margin.

## Marks, opacity and distributions

Use restrained colors on white with enough hue and lightness contrast to distinguish categories. The green-blue-purple presets use distinct first green/blue/purple slots and a wider dark-to-light range; rainbow retains its original RGB values. Follow the [fixed palette contract](palette-contract.md); explicit user color revisions remain supported. For `muted-green-blue-purple`, fill opacity starts around 0.40; base paired scatter around 0.50. Green-blue-purple and rainbow paired scatter with a `-transparent` palette defaults to 0.70 point opacity; other families use their own explicit paired opacity fields. Green-blue-purple paired points additionally use fixed RGB darkening of 0.12; rainbow paired points use 0.00. These renderer-specific settings do not alter distribution or bar colors. Explicit `point_alpha` and `point_darken` override them. The standalone `muted-pastel-six` and `muted-balanced-six` retain fully opaque fills and paired points (1.00) with zero paired RGB darkening. Use `rainbow` for opaque fills (1.00), or `rainbow-transparent` for the earlier translucent appearance (0.40). `muted-green-blue-purple` retains its current 0.40 fill opacity; `muted-green-blue-purple-transparent` provides an additional softer option (0.25). Variants share identical RGB values. Explicit per-figure opacity settings remain valid; clear `fill_alpha` to null when switching a resolved configuration to palette defaults. Distribution points default to opacity 0.55 and a 0.20 darkening of model RGB colors, fixed by the palette contract; do not retune opacity or darkening during layout inspection. A null size resolves to 0.48 mm for modest sample counts or 0.36 mm when the densest retained method/metric group has at least 200 observations. Inspect overlap before enlarging further. Small markers and modest jitter preserve all observations without burying the summaries. Keep line opacity separate from fill opacity.

Box and violin outlines are moderately thicker than their internal marks: start at 0.38 mm for the outline, 0.22 mm for quartile/whisker lines, and 75% of the outline thickness for the median. Use dark gray with about 80% opacity. Box quartiles are solid; violin first/third quartiles remain dashed and stop at the density contour. Both use a solid median. SD overlays are slightly heavier (0.30 mm), with a small white diamond marking the mean. Bar SD stays centered on its method: `combo_sd_display` records both halves or the outward half used to avoid internal-label overlap; the full computed SD remains unchanged in records. Mean comparison lines start at 0.38 mm and include a filled diamond at every method mean (shape 18). Paired reference-difference lines start at 2.3 mm symbol size; standalone ordinary means and facets start at 1.3 mm; dual-axis and shared-axis mean lines start at 2.0 mm. Raw scatter remains absent from lines. Explicit marker overrides are supported. Never confuse quartiles with SD.

Bars default to 0.72 category units wide, leaving a modestly smaller inter-bar gap. Stacked two-metric bar panels use wider 0.84 bars and 0.70 outer category padding: more space beyond the first/last bars and less space between neighboring bars. Adapt width and outer category clearance independently. Boxes retain a separate 0.65 default width. Bars must meet their baseline axis without expansion padding. `axis_policy: auto` selects a zoomed bar range with small padding around the complete displayed values, SD and raw points, rather than always starting at zero. Automatic distribution/line views retain their complete extent. Explicit zero/full/zoom choices override this selection. Eligible automatic positive/negative zoomed bars use `bar_min_fraction: 0.15` as a shortest-bar target, even with external values, subject to zero/domain/baseline constraints; requested internal labels may need additional measured padding. Record and disclose nonzero visible bar baselines; do not interpret truncated lengths as ratios. If SD crosses zero, keep the interval and draw the categorical baseline at zero rather than cropping uncertainty or extending bars to a false baseline.

## Compact ranking, legends and layout

Vertical method comparisons place the best mean on the **left** by default, respecting whether higher or lower is better. Horizontal comparisons retain the reference-style top-to-bottom order. The Performance Ranking arrow always points toward better performance and its arrow/text always remain with the numeric values as one annotation block. When vertical values move below the marks to make room for significance above, move ranking below with them; horizontal values and ranking remain together on the right. Numeric labels remain aligned with their corresponding categories; place them close to the arrow, with enough clearance to avoid touching it. Start around `value_offset: 0.135` for vertical figures and 0.075 for horizontal distributions, adapting to printed dimensions. Horizontal single-metric bars with a ranking arrow use a tighter starting point: `rank_offset: 0.04`, value labels 0.03 axis spans to the right of the line (`value_offset: 0.07` at that default), and `rank_text_gap: 0.04` to its left. Explicit per-figure offsets take precedence. Anchor the ranking text to its non-arrow tail, with a small inward inset and enough clearance for descenders such as `g`. Inspect the actual text size before reducing gaps further. Keep this block close to the plotted marks and axis ends. Do not overlap the title, values or data.

Center titles over the complete figure width, including the ranking annotation gutter, rather than only the data panel or the area remaining after a right margin. Build the layout on an Arial-capable device so measured labels fit the exported canvas.

Metric-colored dual axes use a compact top legend with metric names only. Use a rectangular color block for the bar metric and the same configured mean icon as the plotted line for the line metric, including shape, size and color; the default line icon is a 2.0 mm filled diamond. Do not turn the line key into a second rectangle or add bar/line prefixes. Preserve the selected metric colors and the model-color mode's established subtitle behavior.

Reduce outer margins, category padding, title/axis gaps and inter-panel spacing while retaining the prescribed readable font sizes. Apply category padding only once. For vertical panels, initial category padding is 0.50 for boxes, 0.58 for violins, bar half-width + 0.18, and 0.46 for lines. Respect larger mark widths and adjust for long labels. Many-category horizontal bars should retain a little breathing room above and below the outer bars (about 0.24 category units beyond the bar edge); line panels use less padding while retaining room for the outer numeric annotations. Choose major tick spacing from the final physical axis length and measured Arial label widths. Prefer a coarser readable step (for example 0.1 instead of a crowded 0.05); do not impose a fixed interval. Explicit user breaks override automatic selection but still require visual inspection. Keep every major tick labeled. A compact panel is not permission to crop observations or SD.

Paired categorical color retains a framed internal legend above statistics when its complete content fits safely, otherwise a native framed external guide. Labels stay left-aligned while the complete key/text block is centered; three categories use two above and one centered below. Statistics contain two centered lines without a heading. Measure actual Arial widths on the final square panel: internal statistics and category/size text scale by panel width / `annotation_reference_mm: 40`, from reference sizes 2.2 mm for statistics, 2.0 mm for guide labels and 2.3 mm for guide titles. Automatic `stats_box`, `legend_box` and `size_legend_box` are null; resolved rectangles and scaled fonts are recorded separately from base configuration settings. Statistics reserve 3% horizontal padding per side; categorical guide content reserves 6%. If their automatic widths differ by at most 8% of the larger frame, match widths when readable, collision-free and per-side padding remains at most 10%. Preserve explicit rectangles and refine a too-small frame instead of shrinking text.

Continuous paired color has an independent measured numeric axis. `colorbar_placement: outside` is the default; automatic orientation is vertical/right. Explicit `auto` opts into a readable full internal guide frame, including gradient, title, ticks, background and padding, against point edges, labels, statistics and any internal size guide. Otherwise use the external right-hand axis. Explicit `inside` requires a safe fit; `outside` forces external, vertical/right with auto orientation or below the panel when explicitly horizontal. Automatic horizontal/vertical orientation checks the entire guide, while explicit orientation remains supported. The internal gradient starts at 12 mm, title at 2.0 mm and tick text at 1.6 mm, all scaled by panel width / 40; title wrapping starts at 24 characters so a short covariate title can stay on one line in a safe frame. Grow the gradient length when needed for tick labels and reserve 0.6 mm guide padding, 0.65 mm title gap and 0.45 mm tick gap. External title/ticks retain absolute 1.6 mm, compact 14-character title wrapping and a 0.65 mm panel gap. Explicit legacy title settings apply inside too unless corresponding internal title keys are supplied. Internal color axes use exactly limit minimum, arithmetic midpoint and maximum; exterior axes keep their requested breaks. At each displayed break, use a short inward tick from the label-side boundary (right for vertical gradients, bottom for horizontal): 0.6 mm length and zero offset, proportional inside and absolute outside. Keep ticks shorter than the gradient thickness; draw no full-width separators. The legacy offset key is accepted for saved configurations but does not move ticks outward; record that request and the effective inward geometry. Use the same effective gradient and point opacity in either placement. Keep the complete title and labeled ticks readable; fitting only the gradient is insufficient.

A separate size guide preferably uses a measured framed rectangle above statistics, with hollow circles, ascending labels and a small increasing-size arrow; counts remain integers. Numeric point sizes start at a 0.65–1.9 mm range and optional point labels at 1.9 mm, without inheriting a synthetic example's selected IDs or offsets. At the reference panel its automatic minimum height is 9 mm with 0.65 mm inner padding; scale these with internal text and grow further when content needs it. Its placement is independent of continuous color. Preserve an explicitly requested inside placement, revise geometry when unsafe, and inspect all guide interactions at final size.

Optional paired marginals add pooled X/reference histogram density and KDE above, and pooled Y/focal distributions on the right. They use exactly the retained finite paired sample, including missing visual covariates, and do not change means/tests. Histograms use common edges and density rather than raw-count heights; omit KDE for constant or too-small samples and record why. Start at 8 bins, 6 mm strips and 0.65 mm gaps; the whole canvas includes them while the main scatter remains square. Recalculate proportional annotation sizes from the resulting main panel and keep the selected palette's fixed colors/transform. The `rainbow-transparent` marginal layout resolves omitted/null point opacity to 0.90 and paired RGB darkening to 0.20 for clearer marks and matching guides; explicit/saved values take precedence.

## Adapt after rendering

Style defaults establish consistency; they are not immutable layout coordinates. For each new dataset, assess score ranges, number and length of category names, observation density, and available annotation space. Render and view the actual figure before deciding whether the defaults fit. Adjust the canvas, legend geometry, box dimensions, label sizes, point density treatment, and common axis limits/ticks when needed, then re-render and inspect. Do not reduce legibility merely to retain an inherited rectangle. Keep the final configuration so the adapted result remains reproducible. Use the category’s external guide when the interior cannot accommodate a readable legend without covering data. Ordinary top model/metric guides are unframed; paired statistical and covariate guides retain their separate frame contracts.

## Input and output locations

Input data and figure output may live outside the current workspace. Honor the user's explicit absolute input path and destination directory. Treat the supplied output directory as the parent. Create a dedicated `<figure-name>/` folder for every distinct figure, and set `output_prefix` to `<parent>/<figure-name>/<figure-name>`. Keep all exports and related configurations, statistics, QA, and reproducibility material inside that same folder. Refinements of the same figure reuse its folder unless the user requests a separate version. Do not place loose figure files or a shared cross-figure support folder in the parent. Do not silently substitute a workspace-local destination. A filesystem restriction should be handled through the available approval mechanism for the already requested operation. Keep source data read-only. Preserve unrelated destination files and verify the final paths. When moving existing results, update configuration and reproduction paths, verify the transferred files, and remove the obsolete copies only after a successful transfer.

The current renderer normalizes an ungrouped prefix such as `/project/figures/figure_name` to `/project/figures/figure_name/figure_name`. An already grouped prefix is reused unchanged, so rerunning a saved configuration does not add more nested directories.

## Multiple-method figures and palettes

Use the selected category's distribution and ranking conventions. `rainbow` contains ten muted colors sampled from flat interior fills of a raster reference; these are approximate displayed RGB values. Keep a named model-to-color map across related figures, even when each metric changes the ranking. The red focal/best annotation is independent of the palette. When color encodes methods, follow the [fixed focal/comparator hue policy](palette-contract.md#fixed-categorical-slots): a designated focal method, or exact raw ID `Ours` without an explicit designation, uses a distinct hue family from every automatic comparator. Explicit saved maps remain authoritative; metric colors and continuous covariates retain their own meanings. A best-mean annotation never selects the focal color or statistical hypothesis.

Templates remain adjustable for each real figure. Offer requested alternative displays using the same data handling. Never invent SD for a pooled score or crop distributions to exaggerate a result. If a zoomed bar axis has a nonzero baseline, whether automatic or explicitly selected, record and disclose it; use a full-range distribution view when appropriate.

## Optional focal-method significance

When requested, single-metric raw-data comparisons can run one-sided focal-versus-every-other t-tests. Pair by ID for paired input; use Welch tests for independent input. Fix the focal model and improvement direction before testing. Show compact `p < 0.05` brackets only when every comparison passes; otherwise omit the entire bracket set while retaining every result. Do not omit an inconvenient comparator or infer tests from pooled scores. Bracket spacing uses physical millimeters and must be checked alongside ranking annotations. See [the multiple-model specification](multi-comparison.md#optional-one-sided-significance) for options and dependence assumptions.

With the default `annotation_layout: auto`, both displayed annotation types occupy different sides: horizontal values on the right and significance inside the panel left of the marks; vertical significance above and the complete values/ranking block inside the panel below the marks. The interior strip stays right of the left axis or above the bottom axis, respectively, and clear of observations/uncertainty. Do not place brackets outside the model-name column or values among category tick labels. Only one displayed type uses right/above placement. An explicit `same-side` keeps the legacy arrangement. Significance starts 0.6 mm from the complete displayed extent of the methods spanned by each bracket, including shown observations and SD, with 0.35 mm caps and a 0.4 mm safe internal minimum. Nested spacing starts at 2.7 mm for horizontal significance and 2.2 mm for vertical significance; annotation reservation uses actual Arial glyph bounds. Explicit or saved spacing is respected. A horizontal numeric view need not start at zero: retain complete distribution/SD support and the measured interior strip, while avoiding unrelated empty space. Anchor to that comparison/span rather than the numeric-view boundary or an unrelated method; account for the value/ranking block when annotations share a side. Horizontal significant boxes/violins start at 0.62 category padding, unless explicitly overridden, for extra top/bottom clearance. Preserve all observed values while reserving annotation space and inspect its physical fit.

Measure interior annotation space at export size using Arial. A stated scientific `metric_domain` can guide safe use of an existing empty tail; the observed range alone does not establish that domain. If the tail cannot fit every glyph while retaining observations and SD, extend and disclose the empty annotation view. Preserve the original data limits separately from display limits. Bars retain a flush axis baseline, using labels inside the baseline portion rather than adding a gap. Keep the resolved strip geometry in the per-figure records.

## Complete nearby endpoint ticks

After selecting a readable major-tick interval, extend an automatically chosen numeric axis outward to the next major tick when the remaining gap is at most 20% of that interval. Include and label that tick. Apply this at either end; never shrink a range or crop data/uncertainty to land on a tick. For example, an upper limit of 0.79 with 0.1-spaced ticks extends to 0.8. A lower limit of 0.51 extends to 0.5. A limit of 0.48 must not move inward to 0.5. Explicit requested limits/ticks take precedence. The small outward completion can override the initial zoom-occupancy heuristic. Paired scatter already rounds its shared automatic bounds outward; preserve identical x/y scales.

For shared-row metrics on the same comparable scale, honor requests for identical tick sets and use common limits that contain every value in both panels. A value below the first labeled tick still needs visible axis space. Recheck label spacing at final size after completing ticks. The single-metric and shared-row renderers apply the endpoint rule automatically when both their range and tick choices are automatic.


## Trend and profile conventions

Trend series use a compact top legend with a symbol plus line key matching the marks. Continuous fits default to linear regression; up to two series show a 95% confidence interval for the fitted mean, while larger comparisons omit ribbons by default to reduce overlap. A confidence interval is not a prediction interval. Display at most two scientifically preselected statistic types consistently across every series, and retain all diagnostics at full precision. Never select the annotations or regression form because a result favors a focal model. Polynomial derivatives require a stated x reference rather than being labeled as a constant slope. Dual numeric metric axes must label their original units and preserve both values; visual crossings or relative slopes on transformed axes are not direct quantitative comparisons.

Grouped distributions preserve one model-color map across consecutive comparable metric clusters. Measure top model guides against the complete canvas and anchor them to the whole figure so method-label gutters do not shift them off the page; wrap custom captions using final-size Arial measurements. A broken box axis requires an explicit empty interval, visible break marks and disclosure; keep the full original statistical and plotted-data records. Resolve broken-axis ticks from the compressed physical axis length and actual Arial label bounds, with readable spacing and endpoint clearance. Do not use the gap to hide observations, box/whisker structure or displayed uncertainty. See the selected specifications for restrictions.

Radar profiles retain explicit axis order and established domains. Default to thin model lines and symbols on a white circular panel, with dashed light-gray inner rings and a solid outer perimeter. Explicit `circular-bands` retains optional decorative warm-to-green bands, which are not empirical quantiles or performance thresholds. Optional model polygon fills use the selected palette's unchanged RGB and opacity rules. Prefer a measured framed guide in the circular panel’s empty lower-left or lower-right corner, with an unframed top fallback, and endpoint radial labels; measure optional raw-value labels against all curves and symbols. Do not infer units/domains from observed ranges, fill missing values, or rank polygon area. Label or disclose any direction reversal and per-axis normalization.

## Shared trend and profile appearance

Trend axes follow the existing comparison foundation: gray `#333333` baselines at 0.25 mm, 0.22 mm ticks with 1.3 pt length, Arial and dark text. Continuous comparisons use exactly one icon-line-name row above one shared statistic-header/value row for two series. Do not repeat statistic names beneath every model. Light 95% mean confidence fills use thin colored boundaries. Default fitted lines cover the complete x axis, with out-of-observation-range predictions recorded explicitly as extrapolations; offer the observed-range option for confirmatory displays. Discrete shared-category metric profiles display common row labels only at the left edge or common column labels only on the bottom edge, while retaining the necessary metric-specific numeric scales.

## Ordinary guides in genuine empty space

Choose an interior ordinary legend only after measuring its complete keys, labels and padding at the final physical dimensions. Check plotted point edges, full distribution bodies, bars, whiskers, shown uncertainty, annotations and the panel boundary; preserving whitespace includes a positive physical gap, not merely a zero-overlap test. `grouped-distributions` defaults to `legend_placement: auto`, with `legend_clearance_mm: 0.8`. Its reusable helper searches horizontal, vertical and multiple-column framed guides in data-derived empty regions separately on each export device, selecting the candidate with the broadest surrounding clearance rather than a narrow slit. It conservatively reserves polygon/segment bounds, never alters limits or observations to create a gap, and records chosen coordinates, dimensions, occupancy and fallback reasons. `outside` preserves an unframed top guide with a measured gap from numeric endpoint glyphs; an impossible explicit `inside` fails. Existing paired-guide contracts retain their separate proportional controls. Other categories follow their own supported guide controls after visual inspection. Required top continuous-trend statistical guides retain their two-row alignment even when the panel has free space.

Radar defaults to `muted-green-blue-purple`, with opaque outlines/icons and unchanged base RGB (`line_darken: 0`), on a white circular panel by default; `grid_style: circular-bands` retains decorative bands with `band_alpha: 0.28`. Automatic rainbow mappings retain their specified 0.40 darkening; explicit color maps and saved overrides remain authoritative. Apply effective colors consistently to legend keys and selected value labels, while optional polygon fills retain the base mapping. The guide searches both lower circle corners with actual disk, label and mark clearance and uses an unframed top fallback when no readable interior frame fits. Use the fixed focal/comparator hue mapping, independent of observed performance; never recolor after viewing results or use shading as an empirical threshold.

Long horizontal category labels may use supported modest 15° or 30° rotation, or measured wrapping. Check adjacent label spacing using actual category positions, including grouped metric clusters; do not shrink prescribed type or enlarge the canvas automatically. Preserve explicit angles and verify final glyph bounds and the remaining data panel.

Horizontal distributions reserve category space beyond their full outline, including stroke and axis separation: ordinary boxes start at half box width + 0.24 category units; violins start at 0.64. Retain explicit saved padding, and inspect the physical first/last mark-to-axis gap at final size, especially in dense grouped layouts. Increase supported category padding when actual stroke/marker bounds need it; do not reduce font size or crop the outer distribution.

## Focal order and preview presentation

For categorical method comparisons, place an explicitly requested focal method at the far left in vertical figures and at the bottom in horizontal figures. Use the established improvement direction to order remaining methods. An explicit method order takes precedence; never apply this to ordered scientific categories, continuous covariates or radar axes. The exact raw ID `Ours` is a default visual focal identity for automatic colors, not evidence of better performance, pairing or a statistical hypothesis; infer none of those from the name. Preserve method-to-color identities when reordering.

Radar defaults to a white circular background, dashed inner grid rings and a solid outer perimeter. Requested focal curves can use solid lines over dashed comparators, with original values and declared-domain radii retained. Shared-axis mean comparisons optionally show metric-colored bars beneath the icons; this does not create extra observations. Crowded grouped boxes/violins omit internal mean/SD overlays by default while keeping numerical summaries in records. Explicit overlay requests remain authoritative. A dual-axis mean correlation is descriptive Pearson correlation across matched method means, rather than a paired-observation inference.
